## Evidence and numerical conventions
- Ch14: least squares presented as minimising the residual sum of squares; the slope r·(s_y/s_x) identity, R² = 1 − SSE/SST, and the LINE assumptions are stated with an explicit hand-worked 5-point example (y-hat = 2.2 + 0.6x, R² = 0.6). Granger–Newbold spurious regression and Cook's distance are cited; the population/library and look-ahead stories are labelled teaching schematics, not measurements.
- Ch15: p-value defined as P(data | H0), contrasted with the Fisher and Neyman–Pearson frameworks. ASA's six principles are paraphrased, not quoted. Reproducibility figures come from Open Science Collaboration (2015) and Camerer et al. (2016); the uniform-p-value and file-drawer figures are labelled schematics; fail-safe N is attributed to Rosenthal (1979) with its one-tailed 2.706 constant noted.
- Ch16: FWER = 1 − (1 − α)^m tables are exact; Bonferroni and Benjamini–Hochberg are distinguished (FWER vs FDR). The Simpson figure uses constructed data that reverses under aggregation, while the Berkeley case is attributed to Bickel et al. (1975). The Wald survivorship story cites Mangel & Samaniego (1984). The genetic-significance order of magnitude is derived as α/m.
- Ch17: confounding (HRT vs WHI) attributed to the 2002 JAMA trial; DAG, backdoor criterion and the do-operator follow Hernán & Robins and Pearl (2019). The interactive and code models state their linear form so the naive/adjusted slopes are reproducible.
- Ch18: misconduct cases described from public retraction records without quoting disputed retraction counts; the Wakefield retraction, Fanelli (2009), Registered Reports, OSF/ClinicalTrials.gov and FAIR principles are cited. The selective-reporting widget is expectation arithmetic under stated independence and power assumptions.

Every chapter has `evidence/NN.json` distinguishing verified sources (each with a source_url) from derived or illustrative entries.
